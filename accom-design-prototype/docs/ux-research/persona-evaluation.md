# Persona Evaluation

These are hypothetical, research-informed test personas. The findings below are predicted friction, not user-testing results.

## A — International first-year student

- **Goals:** secure a legitimate, affordable room before arriving; understand the full commitment and available help.
- **Anxieties:** visa changes, deposits, hidden bills, unfamiliar terms, remote support and whether images are representative.
- **Essential information:** operator identity, total rent, upfront payments, included bills, dates, visa/cancellation terms and next-step status.
- **Tasks:** compare two rooms; calculate total rent; check bills/cancellation; submit an enquiry.
- **Barriers:** weekly price hides total commitment; policy links and “book” language can imply certainty.
- **Confidence builders:** dated source labels, provider identity, clear unknowns, itemised totals and explicit pending confirmation.
- **Challenge:** Photography and a starting price do not answer “Can I trust this, and what if plans change?”
- **Design response:** repeat dates and total beside the selected room and CTA; state that enquiry is pending and takes no payment.

## B — UK undergraduate

- **Goals:** quickly find an affordable room with the desired social life and a workable commute.
- **Anxieties:** paying more for little value, missing better options and photos that obscure shared/private space.
- **Essential information:** comparable prices, room-specific photos, shared spaces, university walk and bathroom/kitchen arrangement.
- **Tasks:** filter room types; distinguish ensuite/studio; compare two rooms; find campus travel.
- **Barriers:** long property copy and scattered details force back-and-forth memory.
- **Confidence builders:** concise room rows, consistent comparison schema and commute facts with source/mode.
- **Challenge:** The property story must never push the room decision below promotional content.
- **Design response:** rooms follow the overview immediately; two-room comparison co-locates price, privacy, kitchen and tenancy.

## C — Postgraduate student

- **Goals:** find a private, quiet, study-suitable room with workable contract flexibility.
- **Anxieties:** noise, unreliable Wi-Fi, ambiguous room size and social-space marketing replacing practical detail.
- **Essential information:** dimensions, desk/study space, private/shared configuration, Wi-Fi and tenancy options.
- **Tasks:** compare ensuite/studio; inspect room details; switch durations; identify quiet-study facilities.
- **Barriers:** generic amenities cannot establish room fit; features may not belong to the selected room.
- **Confidence builders:** room-level specifications and explicit confirmed/unknown labels.
- **Challenge:** “Vibrant student life” cannot be the only lifestyle presented.
- **Design response:** privacy and kitchen lead the comparison; study space is visible; unsupported noise claims are omitted.

## D — Parent or guarantor

- **Goals:** understand financial exposure, security, tenancy obligations and support.
- **Anxieties:** headline “from” prices, hidden fees, unclear cancellation and when a commitment begins.
- **Essential information:** total rent, booking fee treatment, dates, payment/guarantor conditions, security and current policies.
- **Tasks:** find total rent; inspect policies; verify the next step and booking status.
- **Barriers:** dense legal text after the CTA and unexplained weekly prices.
- **Confidence builders:** plain-language summaries linked to current full terms, itemised cost and explicit payee/status language.
- **Challenge:** Conversion must not come before obligations can be evaluated.
- **Design response:** total commitment is persistent; policy uncertainty is visible before the CTA; enquiry takes no money.

## E — Student with accessibility requirements

- **Goals:** determine whether the property and a specific room meet access needs, then ask follow-up questions independently.
- **Anxieties:** vague “accessible” badges, inaccessible controls and missing route/room measurements.
- **Essential information:** step-free route, lift, doorway/bathroom details, accessible room availability and contact method.
- **Tasks:** navigate by keyboard/screen reader/mobile assistive technology; locate access details; send an access enquiry.
- **Barriers:** image-led pages cannot establish physical accessibility; unknowns may be silently omitted.
- **Confidence builders:** verified property/room facts, explicit unknowns and a dedicated accessible enquiry route.
- **Challenge:** Digital accessibility is necessary but cannot substitute for property-level access data.
- **Design response:** dedicated access panel, accessible controls/dialogs, no unverified access claim, topic-specific enquiry.

## Task-scenario friction review

| Scenario | Predicted friction | Implemented improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Compare two room types | Remembering facts across expanded cards | Two-room comparison table with the same rows |
| Find total rent | Weekly price disguises commitment | Weekly × selected weeks with an illustrative total beside each option and CTA |
| Ensuite vs studio | Rental jargon is unfamiliar | One-tap plain-language explanation and comparison rows |
| University travel | A map pin lacks mode/context | Operator-published walking times, named destinations and schematic route disclaimer |
| Cancellation conditions | Long policy text is skipped | Concise accordion summary that says when full current terms are required |
| Bills included | Property amenities and bill inclusions blur together | Included, paid separately and confirm-first columns |
| Correct next step | Competing enquiry/book labels | One primary “Enquire” action throughout |
| Enquiry confirmed or pending | Success can be mistaken for a reservation | Success state says pending, no room held and no booking confirmed |

## Future validation

Recruit representative students for moderated task testing, include screen-reader/keyboard users, and test with current property data. Measure task success, comprehension and error recovery; do not reuse these predicted findings as results.
